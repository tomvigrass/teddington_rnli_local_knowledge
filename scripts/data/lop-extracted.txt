The Locks

Richmond Lock and Weir
The weir comprises three vertical steel sluice gates suspended from a
footbridge. Each gate weighs 32.6 tonnes and is 20 metres wide and
3.64 metres in depth.
For around two hours each side of high tide, the sluice gates are raised
into the footbridge structure above, allowing boats to pass through the
barrage. At these times, 2 amber lights are shown, day and night, at
each arch - weir open, free navigation.
For the rest of the day, the sluice gates are closed and passing river
traffic must use the lock alongside the barrage. The lock is 76m long
and 8m wide and is manned 24/7. When the sluice gates are down and
the weir closed, there will be three red discs in the form of an inverted
triangle shown at each arch by day, and an inverted triangle of three
red lights will be shown by night.
The period of free navigation can be dramatically changed due to
prevailing conditions. In drought conditions, the gates will be closed for
longer periods, whilst in periods of high fluvial flow they may remain
open for much longer.
For current information, contact the Lock Foreman on 0208 940 0634.
An exclusion zone is established from the extremities of the Lock
Islands in a line across the river to the Middlesex shore. Mariners
should not enter this zone whilst the weirs are in the lowered position
or when they are being operated. Only when the weir gates are fully
raised and the Closed Arch Signs have been removed is it permitted to
navigate into the navigation exclusion zone and through Richmond
Lock Footbridge No. 2 or No. 3 arches. Only those vessels
manoeuvring on or to the Lock island berths, vessels engaged in
emergency operations or smaller vessels wishing to utilise the boat
rollers in No. 1 arch are exempt from this.
In order to carry out essential maintenance works on Richmond lock,
weirs and sluices, the weirs at Richmond are lifted for a period of
approximately 3 weeks in October/November every year. This allows
the river between Richmond Lock and Teddington Lock to drain down
at low water on each tide. During this period, the river will NOT be
maintained at the nominal level of 1.72m above Ordnance Datum
Newlyn and the river bed will be exposed at low water.

Teddington Lock
Consists of a large, long weir and three locks: a conventional launch
lock in regular use, the large barge lock and a small skiff lock. The
barge lock was made to accommodate long barges, steamers
or passenger ferries and has an additional set of gates half-way to
operate more quickly for shorter craft. The staggered structures
incorporate two reinforced narrow islands. The upper island is
traversed by and accessible by the lock gates or Teddington Lock
Footbridge.
Manned 24/7. Where not manned for any reason, refer to applicable
Teddington LOP re Lock Operation.
The weir presents a very significant hazard, particularly in flood
conditions, and considerable care needs to be taken to avoid being
drawn into the sluices.

flows (where the flow is increased because of higher fluvial (non-tidal)

With each flag warning, the following advice will be issued, which all
recreational craft users are required to consider carefully before
venturing onto the tidal Thames:
Extreme Caution – EBB TIDE Very Strong Fluvial Flows
RED

Fluvial flows are very strong. Conditions are
difficult/dangerous.
All man-powered vessels advised not to go afloat on the ebb
tide.
Caution – EBB TIDE Strong Fluvial Flows

All river users of man-powered vessels should navigate with
extreme caution & consider whether it is safe for them to go
YELLOWafloat on the ebb tide.
PLA advise man-powered vessels - in particular schools,
novices, junior crews - or those that do not usually use the
tidal Thames not to go afloat on the ebb tide.
Average Fluvial Flows
GREEN

All river users to navigate with caution and maintain a proper
look out.

In addition, there will be a fourth level for all river users to note, which
is when there are low fluvial flows:
Caution - Low Fluvial Flows
BLACK

Tidal flow is lower than usual. River users should
expect lower than predicted tides especially around
low water.

ENVIRONMENT AGENCY WARNING SYSTEM
Warning boards may be displayed at locks to inform river users of the
conditions.
When red boards (CAUTION STRONG STREAM) are displayed on
lock gates, they advise users of all boats not to navigate because the
strong flows make it difficult and dangerous.
When yellow boards (CAUTION STREAM INCREASING) are
displayed on lock gates, they advise users of all unpowered boats not
to navigate and users of powered boats to find a safe mooring. This is
because river flows are likely to strengthen and red boards could be
displayed very soon and without warning.
When yellow boards (CAUTION STREAM DECREASING) are
displayed on lock gates, they advise users of all unpowered boats not
to navigate and users of powered boats to navigate with caution.
For updated information on warning boards, see –
http://riverconditions.environment-agency.gov.uk/

FOG
Fog can have a very significant effect on operations in some areas,
particularly in the area of Eel Pie Island and then between
Hammerton’s Pier and Glover’s Island. There is also a tendency for
small but dense banks of fog to form throughout our area. Extreme
caution is required in these conditions.
